AXIS ACADEMY

Learn to think in motion.

A practical introduction to robots, coordinates, and teaching your first movement.

01

Meet a robot

A robot combines a mechanism, actuators, sensors, and a controller. The controller turns a program into movement. Axis models a six-joint arm: base, shoulder, elbow, and three wrist joints. Six joint angles describe its pose.

Try Joint controls. Move one slider at a time and watch which part rotates. Home position restores a known pose.

02

Coordinates and reach

Axis uses millimeters for tool coordinates. Y points up; X and Z span the table. Joint angles use degrees from neutral. Inverse kinematics finds joint angles for a requested tool position. Some points are outside the arm’s reach or joint limits.

Read the end-effector coordinates beneath the scene. Drag the blue tool handle horizontally, then adjust Tool height. An unreachable target leaves the last valid pose in place.

03

Pick, move, place

Approach an object from above, lower the tool to its center, close the gripper, lift, move, lower, and release. A grasp attaches a nearby object to the tool. Releasing lets gravity return it to a surface.

Open Joint controls to close or open the gripper. Watch Payload to confirm a grasp. Arm parts push scene objects using conservative box contacts; objects use round proxies. This is simplified contact behavior, not a dynamics or torque model.

04

Teach a path

Hand teaching lets you create a sequence without writing coordinates. Move the blue handle or joint sliders and click Record point. Each click captures coordinates, all six angles, gripper state, and elapsed time.

Capture at least two points, then Play path. Playback interpolates joint angles between poses; tool motion may curve between recorded coordinates. Capture timing requests the duration, but motor speed limits can extend it. Stop interrupts movement. Paths persist in this browser; Clear path removes them.

05

Design your arm

Longer links change reach. Joint limits constrain available poses. Gearing reduces output speed and servo travel. Stepper settings determine angular resolution. These tradeoffs affect which programs a build can execute.

Change dimensions or motors in Build arm and Apply build. Rerecord paths after changing geometry: old joint angles can produce different coordinates. Export JSON to keep a copy of the build.

06

Your first Arduino sketch

setup() runs once. loop() repeats until you press Stop. Servo.h controls supported servo joints, and the virtual Axis.h library provides coordinated movement and gripper commands.

Read the Arduino examples next. Axis interprets a supported C++ subset in the browser. Axis.h is a simulator API; a real arm needs a hardware implementation, calibrated limits, and appropriate motor drivers.
